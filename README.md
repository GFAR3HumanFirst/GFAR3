# GFAR³

## Human First. We Not Me. The Future Remains Open.

**GFAR³ is a human-centered framework for examining progress while preserving humanity's ability to choose what comes next.**

> **The greatest progress does not determine our future. It keeps our future ours.**

We can ask whether something can be built, implemented, scaled, automated, governed, or made successful.

GFAR³ asks us to go further.

**Should we do it?**  
**What happens if it succeeds?**  
**What happens if we are wrong?**  
**Can we recover?**  
**Can future generations understand it, question it, change it, refuse it, leave it, or replace it?**

And ultimately:

## Does the Future Remain Open?

---

## GFAR³ Is a Framework, Not a Doctrine

GFAR³ is not a religion, political ideology, final moral code, or unquestionable creed. It claims no moral infallibility.

It is intended to remain open to **questioning, criticism, correction, improvement, modification, and replacement**.

Participation does not require agreement with GFAR³.

## HELP US BREAK THIS.

**What are we missing? Help us improve it.**

GFAR³ itself must remain subject to the same examination it asks us to apply to everything else.

**GFAR³ Is Subject to GFAR³.**

# LEARN IT → TEST IT → BREAK IT → REPORT IT

## LEARN IT

Understand the questions GFAR³ asks and why they matter.

GFAR³ begins with the original Eight-Question GFAR Test:

1. **Human Dignity**
2. **Freedom**
3. **Opportunity**
4. **Truth & Transparency**
5. **Human Agency**
6. **Fairness**
7. **Future Generations**
8. **Human Progress**

These questions begin the examination. They do not end it.

## TEST IT

Choose a consequential decision, idea, technology, policy, institution, system, proposal, or even GFAR³ itself, and put it through the framework.

### The Success Test

## NOW ASSUME IT SUCCEEDS.

Do not examine only whether something works as intended. Ask what happens **because it succeeds**.

What becomes better? What becomes worse? Who gains power? Who bears risk or sacrifice? What alternatives could disappear? Can humanity still change course?

### The Recovery Test

## NOW ASSUME WE COULD BE WRONG.

Humans can be wrong. AI can be wrong. Institutions can be wrong. GFAR³ can be wrong.

Consequential decisions should therefore be examined not only for whether they appear right today, but for whether harm can be:

**Prevented → Detected → Contained → Understood → Interrupted → Reversed → Recovered From → Learned From**

Recovery does not mean refusing to act. **Failure to act can also cause harm and close the future.** Action and inaction both have consequences.

> **For this test, disagreement, criticism, opposition, or dissent should not by themselves be treated as harm.**

As potential harm increases and reversibility decreases, the justification, evidence, safeguards, independent review, and appropriate human oversight expected should increase accordingly.

This is part of the **Right to Recover**.

# DREAM BIG & THE FUTURE TEST

Avoiding failure is not enough.

## DREAM BIG.

Think beyond what could go wrong. **Ask what could go right.**

Think of someone you love. Imagine the world that person, and generations not yet born, could inherit if humanity uses knowledge, technology, resources, creativity, and capability wisely.

Then ask the harder question:

## Why shouldn't every child inherit that same opportunity?

Good ancestors should not try to determine the final future. They should leave future generations the dignity, freedom, knowledge, opportunity, agency, and ability to recover and change course that they will need to build their own.

Can future generations **understand it, question it, change it, refuse or leave it, and replace it** if something better becomes possible?

What are we locking in? What are we leaving possible?

## Does the Future Remain Open?

# BREAK IT

After testing the original subject, turn the examination around.

## Test GFAR³ itself.

Look for contradictions, missing principles, hidden assumptions, manipulation risks, unintended consequences, people or perspectives the framework fails to represent, circumstances where following GFAR³ could produce a worse outcome, and reasons GFAR³ itself may be wrong.

**Criticism is part of the test.**

Make the strongest reasonable argument you can. Do not weaken criticism simply because it challenges the framework.

---

# Human First

**Human First does not mean Human Only.**

It does not mean humans are automatically right, morally infallible, or entitled to domination.

It means systems created, deployed, or governed by humanity should remain accountable to humanity and to the human future affected by them.

Capability is not the same thing as legitimate authority.

---

# We Not Me

## WE INCLUDES PEOPLE WHO DISAGREE WITH US.

“We” cannot mean only those who agree with us.

People may disagree with, question, criticize, oppose, or challenge an idea, including GFAR³ itself, and remain part of the human “we.”

**Disagreement is not automatically harm.**  
**Criticism is not betrayal.**  
**Opposition does not make someone less human.**  
**Dissent does not place someone outside “we.”**

> **A framework intended to serve humanity must remain capable of including humanity even when humanity disagrees.**

---

# Power → Responsibility, Not Entitlement

Greater capability should not automatically produce greater entitlement. Greater power should bring greater responsibility and scrutiny.

Ask:

**Who benefits? Who sacrifices? Who decides?**

Distinguish between **voluntary sacrifice and imposed sacrifice**. The good of the many does not automatically make the few disposable.

No consequential system should depend solely on the virtue of those who control it. This is why GFAR³ also examines the possibility of the **Corruptible Guardian**.

---

# Good Ancestors

GFAR³ does not ask us to design the final human civilization.

It asks whether we can leave those who come after us enough **dignity, freedom, knowledge, opportunity, agency, recoverability, and possibility** to build theirs.

## May humanity live long and prosper.

# Where GFAR³ May Be Wrong

Serious criticism should not disappear merely because GFAR³ survives it.

As public testing proceeds, this project should preserve a public record of significant criticism, including:

- the strongest reasonable criticism
- GFAR³'s response
- changes made
- changes not made
- why
- unresolved disagreements
- unanswered questions

A criticism does not have to defeat GFAR³ to deserve preservation. GFAR³ should not have the final word merely because the criticism is about GFAR³.
